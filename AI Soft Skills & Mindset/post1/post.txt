AI literacy is the new Excel.

Five years ago Excel skill meant you could get better job. Today same is happening with AI literacy.

It is not about coding. It is about knowing what AI can do, what it cannot, where it fails, what it costs.

4 parts of AI literacy that actually matter for non technical team👇

1. What AI can do today, not hype:

> Before: Think AI can do everything, get disappointed
> After: AI is good at - summarization, drafting first version, research with sources, extracting points, connecting apps.
> Example: Research with Perplexity, clean notes with Claude, draft with ChatGPT, log with Make.

2. What AI cannot do, where it fails:

> Before: Trust AI output blindly, ship wrong info
> After: AI fails at - recent events after cutoff, numbers it is not confident about, attributing quotes, inventing sources.
> Rule: If not fully certain, say so clearly. Do not state guesses as facts.

3. What AI costs in time and money:

> Before: Think AI is free and instant
> After: Each token costs, each long output needs full forward pass, hallucinations happen when confident wrong prediction becomes next input.
> Lesson: More context is not better context. Focused 2000 tokens beats bloated 8000.

4. What you should never automate:

> Before: Automate everything, lose human touch
> After: Never automate final decision, final email without human check, relationship building. Automate draft, not final voice.
> Rule: Automate 80 percent boring steps, keep 20 percent human voice.

AI literacy is not prompt engineering. It is knowing where AI helps and where it hurts.

Leaders who understand AI will make better decisions. Leaders who do not will depend on vendors.

You do not need to learn coding. You need to understand these 4 parts.

Save this if you lead a non technical team.

Small win: Learn what AI cannot do before what it can.
